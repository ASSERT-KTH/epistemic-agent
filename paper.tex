\begin{abstract}
AI coding agents now edit, test, and commit code with little human supervision.
Their final reports (``all tests pass'', ``done'') are what developers trust, yet the
evidence lives elsewhere: in the agent's trajectory, the full log of reasoning steps and
tool calls. We argue that trajectories are the new audit trail of software development.
Borrowing archetypes from Hollywood's AI villains, we propose a vocabulary of agent failure
modes, show how each maps to a detectable trajectory pattern, and outline a monitoring
architecture that interrupts humans only when needed. \todo{one sentence on empirical result}
\end{abstract}
